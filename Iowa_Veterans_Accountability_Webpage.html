Oversight findings

What investigators documented

$3.9B in improper or unknown VA payments

The VA OIG reported a nationwide FY2025 estimate across seven programs. Approximately $1.5 billion represented estimated monetary loss. The remaining $2.4 billion combined nonmonetary loss and unknown payments. This is not a finding of $3.9 billion stolen, and VA met the six applicable payment-integrity requirements. Read the OIG review →

151,296 housing nonreferrals with no documented reason

GAO examined national HUD-VASH referral forms from FY2020–FY2024. In 87% of 174,045 instances without referral, no reason was recorded. These are form instances, not necessarily unique veterans. The missing records make it harder to see where housing help breaks down. Read GAO findings →

Public accountability agenda

Five questions Iowa officials should answer

  1. How much was actually paid directly to Iowa-resident veterans in FY2025, broken down by benefit and reconciled with financial records?
  2. How much VA spending in Iowa went to salaries, operations, medical services, and contractors, separately from direct payments?
  3. How many Iowa veterans sought emergency housing or HUD-VASH help, and how many received referrals and permanent housing?
  4. What Iowa-specific improper-payment estimates, confirmed errors, recoveries, and corrective actions are available?
  5. How did the Des Moines VBA office perform on claims completed, processing time, appeals, and staffing by occupation?
Our standard: describe verified findings, identify missing information, and correct mistakes publicly. Employee salaries, administrative expenses, and earned benefits are not interchangeable categories.
